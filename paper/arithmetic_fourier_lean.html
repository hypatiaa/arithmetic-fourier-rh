Abstract

We attach a unit phase to each prime and multiply those phases according to the factorization of an integer. For each fixed integer modulus B≥1B \ge 1, this defines a Möbius-weighted trigonometric polynomial DX,BD_{X,B} whose mean-square size under the product Poisson measure ν\nu (radius 1/p1/p at each prime pp) can be computed explicitly: ∥DX,B∥ν2=∑r≤X(r,B)=1j(r)SB(⌊X/r⌋)2,j(r)=∏p∣r(1−1p2),SB(t)=∑k≤t(k,B)=1M(t/k)k,\|D_{X,B}\|_\nu^2 = \sum_{\substack{r \le X \\ (r,B)=1}} j(r)\, S_B(\lfloor X/r \rfloor)^2, \qquad j(r) = \prod_{p \mid r} \left(1 - \frac{1}{p^2}\right), \qquad S_B(t) = \sum_{\substack{k \le t \\ (k,B)=1}} \frac{M(t/k)}{k}, where M(t)=∑1≤n≤tμ(n)M(t) = \sum_{1 \le n \le t} \mu(n) is the Mertens function. The Riemann Hypothesis is equivalent to ||DX,B||ν2 = OB,δ(X1+δ) for every δ > 0, to two-sided and one-sided bounds on SB, and in scaled form to zero-free half-planes Re s > a (½ ≤ a < 1). 1. Why a Mean Square Can Carry the Riemann Hypothesis

Write ℰB(X) for the right-hand side of the mean-square identity in the Abstract. Four short steps explain why bounding this single sum of squares captures the full content of the Riemann Hypothesis (RH).

The energy controls the scalar. At r = 1 the weight is j(1) = 1, so the first term of the sum is SB(X)2. Every other term is non-negative, giving SB(X)2 ≤ ℰB(X).

The scalar controls the energy. If |SB(t)| ≤ A tθ for some θ > ½, then each term is at most A2(X/r)2θ, and since 0 ≤ j(r) ≤ 1, ℰB(X)≤A2∑r≥1r−2θX2θ=A2ζ(2θ)X2θ.\mathscr{E}_B(X) \le A^2 \sum_{r \ge 1} r^{-2\theta}\, X^{2\theta} = A^2\, \zeta(2\theta)\, X^{2\theta}. Hence SB(n) ≪ε n1/2+ε for every ε > 0 holds if and only if ℰB(X) ≪δ X1+δ for every δ > 0.

The scalar is the Mertens function in disguise. Put gB(k) = 1/k when gcd(k, B) = 1 and 0 otherwise, and hB(k) = μ(k) gB(k). Because gB is completely multiplicative, gB * hB = δ1 under Dirichlet convolution, yielding the exact two-way inversion SB(n)=∑k=1ngB(k)M(⌊n/k⌋),M(n)=∑k=1nhB(k)SB(⌊n/k⌋).S_B(n) = \sum_{k=1}^n g_B(k)\, M(\lfloor n/k \rfloor), \qquad M(n) = \sum_{k=1}^n h_B(k)\, S_B(\lfloor n/k \rfloor). Both kernels satisfy |gB(k)|, |hB(k)| ≤ 1/k, so any power bound passes through either sum unchanged: M(n) = O(nθ) ⇔ SB(n) = O(nθ) for every θ > 0.

The classical step. By Littlewood [1912], RH holds if and only if M(x) = Oε(x1/2+ε) for every ε > 0 [Titchmarsh 1986; Montgomery–Vaughan 2007]. Putting the four steps together gives RH⇔M≪εx1/2+ε⇔SB≪εn1/2+ε⇔ℰB(X)≪δX1+δ⇔∥DX,B∥ν2≪δX1+δ.\mathrm{RH} \iff M \ll_\varepsilon x^{1/2+\varepsilon} \iff S_B \ll_\varepsilon n^{1/2+\varepsilon} \iff \mathscr{E}_B(X) \ll_\delta X^{1+\delta} \iff \|D_{X,B}\|_\nu^2 \ll_\delta X^{1+\delta}. That is why a question about the norm of a trigonometric polynomial carries the full content of RH. One sign is already enough: using the coprime Dirichlet factor L(gB, s) and Landau's positivity theorem [Landau 1905; Mertens 1897], a lower bound σSB(n) ≥ −Cεn1/2+ε for either fixed sign σ ∈ {1, −1} is also equivalent to RH, and shifting the exponent a ∈ [½, 1) covers zero-free half-planes Re s > a. 7. Prior Art and Related Work

To place both the mathematical construction and its Lean 4 formalization cleanly in the surrounding literature, we separate what was already known from what this paper and companion artifact contribute.

7.1 Analytic Prior Art: GCD Sums and Infinite-Polydisc Poisson Integrals

The covariance kernel underlying Proposition 2.5(1) is classical in the theory of GCD matrices and Dirichlet-series Hilbert spaces. Hedenmalm, Lindqvist, and Seip [1997] introduced the Hilbert space ℋ2 of Dirichlet series via the Bohr lift to the infinite-dimensional polydisc, Lindqvist and Seip [1998] analyzed associated GCD quadratic forms, and Hilberdink [2009] linked spectral norms of arithmetic GCD matrices to Ω-bounds for ζ(s). Building on this line of work, Aistleitner, Berkes, and Seip [2015] identified general GCD sums as Poisson integrals on the infinite polydisc evaluated at radial weights (p−α):

∑k,ℓ=1..N gcd(nk, nℓ)2α / (nknℓ)α (0 < α ≤ 1)  ↔  Proposition 2.5(1) at α = 1: ⟨Z(d), Z(e)⟩ν = gcd(d, e)2/(de).

Against that backdrop, the contribution developed here enters after the α = 1 Poisson kernel is fixed:

  1. Coprime Möbius-lift polynomial: Combining the prime-phasor monomials Z(n) with the Möbius lift An and the inclusion–exclusion sieving window wX,B (B ≥ 1) produces the finite trigonometric polynomial DX,B(z) = ∑n≤X, (n,B)=1 wX,B(n) An(z), whose monomial coefficients collapse exactly to the standard Mertens coefficients M(X/d) (Proposition 2.2).
  2. Gram–Schmidt diagonalization: Applying explicit prime-power Gram–Schmidt modes Ψr = ∏pa∥r (zpa − p−1 zpa−1) with orthogonal norms ⟨Ψr, Ψs⟩ν = 1r=s j(r) (j(r) = ∏p|r (1 − p−2)) collapses the L2(ν) squared norm directly to the coherent energy ℰB(X) = ||DX,B||ν2 = ∑r≤X, (r,B)=1 j(r) SB(⌊X/r⌋)2 (Proposition 2.5).
  3. Equivalences for RH and zero-free strips: Coupling Proposition 2.5 with the exact coprime Dirichlet inversion gB * hB = δ1 and non-vanishing Euler factor L(gB, s) establishes bidirectional two-sided and Landau-type one-sided equivalences with RH and zero-free half-planes across every fixed modulus B (Theorems 5.1–5.3 and CoherentEnergyRHBridge).

7.2 Formalization Prior Art in Lean 4

In Lean 4 and Mathlib, foundational definitions of ζ(s), Dirichlet L-functions, and RiemannHypothesis were formalized by Loeffler and Stoll [2025]. Several Lean projects investigate alternative RH formulations: Gomes and Kontorovich (AlexKontorovich/Lean-RH) formalized a Dirichlet-η equivalence, and KXKXKXK/riemann-hypothesis-lean formalizes critical-strip and zero-counting equivalences. 8. Scope, Limitations, and Open Directions

  1. A verified equivalence dictionary, not a proof of RH.
    Every main theorem in Sections 3–5 proves an "if and only if" (or conditional implication) between RH\mathrm{RH} / a zero-free half-plane and a growth bound on SBS_B or ℰB\mathscr{E}_B. Obtaining an unconditional estimate ℰB(X)≪δX1+δ\mathscr{E}_B(X) \ll_\delta X^{1+\delta} or σSB(n)≥−Cεn1/2+ε\sigma S_B(n) \ge -C_\varepsilon n^{1/2+\varepsilon} is as hard as proving the Riemann Hypothesis itself.
  2. Relation to classical literature.
    In analytic content, the two-sided and one-sided criteria belong to the classical lineage of Mertens [1897], Landau [1905], and Littlewood [1912], while the infinite-polydisc Poisson covariance stands in the GCD-sum lineage culminating in Aistleitner–Berkes–Seip [2015] (Section 7). This work combines that Poisson covariance with the coprime Möbius lift DX,B, orthogonal system {Ψr}, energy reduction ℰB(X), and a unified, kernel-replayed Lean 4 formalization over Mathlib.
  3. Open formalization and computational directions.
    • Formalize the analytic closed-span complement example (z̄p − p−1 ⊥ Z(n) in L2(Ω, ν)) noted after Proposition 2.5.
    • Extend independent statement review beyond the principal equivalences of v2.6.4 and the headline statements of CoherentEnergyMeasure / CoherentEnergyRHBridge to auxiliary compensated-trace modules and internal helper lemmas.
